## Unreleased — Options: narrower wing when the usual one is too big for the per-trade risk budget (paper only)

- The options strategies place the protective leg of a credit spread a fixed $10 from the short strike. Aggressive risks about 2% of the options pool per trade, so on a small account one contract of a $10 wing can lose more than the whole per-trade budget (on 2026-11-10: a budget of about $428 against a typical max loss of about $768), the position sizer rounds to zero contracts, and the candidate is discarded: 1,479 of 2,082 scan rejections that day were "sized to 0 contracts". For bull put and bear call spreads, in paper trading only, the scan now looks for a narrower wing ($5, $2.50, $1) on the same short strike when, and only when, the usual wing would size to zero, takes the widest one whose max loss fits the budget, and prices it from the option chain it has already fetched (no extra broker request). The risk budget itself is unchanged and is the same cap the sizer already enforced: this changes how much is risked per contract, not how much may be risked per trade. Spreads whose usual wing already fits are untouched. A narrowed spread must still be a listed strike with a usable quote, pay a real credit of at least 10% of its width, and then passes every existing gate unchanged (AI scorer, equity-signal conflict check, frequency controller including the 0.70 confidence floor, probability-of-profit and risk-score limits, guardrails, the 5-position cap, market hours). Because a narrower wing pays a smaller credit its reward-to-risk is low, so the controller's risk-score limit still refuses most low-confidence candidates; this removes one filter, not all of them, and it does not by itself make trades likely. Narrowed signals carry `width_fitted: true` on their spread so they can be told apart. Iron condors, debit spreads and live accounts are not affected. Needs a backend restart; no migration, no new environment variables.
