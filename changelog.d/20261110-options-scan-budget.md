## Unreleased — Options scan: time-boxed, bounded backlog, and candidates are dispatched before the deadline

- Every options scan was running out the scheduler's 240-second limit and being cancelled before it dispatched anything: option-chain requests averaged about 27 seconds each (20–70 seconds for liquid names), only 4 of the IBKR queue's workers serve background scans, and a full pass over the 102-symbol watchlist needs roughly three times the time it gets. Qualifying signals were still recorded, but none was sent on for execution, and requests kept running after the scan gave up on them, so work piled up. The scan now keeps its own budget: it stops starting new symbols after 150 seconds, stops waiting after 200, and dispatches whatever qualified (ranked, best first) before 232, always inside the scheduler limit. At most 4 scan jobs are outstanding at once, counted until the worker really finishes the job (not until the scan stops waiting), and a job whose scan has already given up is skipped when a worker reaches it instead of running for nobody. Symbols not reached are deferred and the next cycle starts where this one stopped, so the same tickers are not always the ones skipped; with today's request speed a full pass takes several cycles. Each cycle logs "N of 102 symbol(s) returned a result in Xs (… not reached; next cycle starts at …)", and a symbol that takes 10 seconds or more logs its per-step timings (price bars, portfolio state, live chain quote, AI scorer, account value, risk state) so a slow name can be explained. Every dispatched candidate still goes through exactly the same frequency-controller, guardrail, portfolio and execution gates; no limit or threshold changed, and the shared IBKR request queue is untouched. Needs a backend restart; no migration, no new environment variables.
