# ADR-0002 — Legal operating model and regulated scope

- **Status:** Open — awaiting qualified securities counsel. **This is not an engineering decision and must not be closed by one.**
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Decides:** `MASTER_ARCHITECTURE.md` §23 item 1 and §2.2; first entry in §20.2's required list
- **Baseline:** `main` at `d933bfd`
- **Blocks:** §21 Phase 2 action 2 (routing a customer's orders through their own connection), Phase 4 (limited-live canary), and all customer live trading

---

## Why this record exists

§2.2 requires that, before customer live trading, counsel and compliance
classify the intended service. Engineering cannot substitute for that
determination, and this ADR does not attempt to: **it contains no
recommendation and proposes no answer.**

What it does contain is a precise description of what the software actually
does, verified against `main` rather than against intent. Counsel cannot
classify a system they have to infer. Supplying that description accurately is
the part engineering can do, and getting it wrong in either direction —
overstating the automation or understating it — produces the wrong
classification just as surely as not asking.

## What the system does today, verified against the code

### It is non-custodial

No customer cash or securities are held. Orders are placed into the customer's
own brokerage account using credentials the customer supplies. Custody,
clearing and settlement are explicit non-goals (§2.3).

### Credentials

A customer supplies an Alpaca API key pair. It is encrypted at rest with
Fernet (`services/credential_cipher.py`), stored per organization
(`broker_connections`), and revocable. Only the last four characters of the key
are held in clear, so a listing screen never decrypts anything.

### Signals are generated by the system, not by the customer

`equity_signal_engine`, `strategy_engine`, `meta_strategy` and related services
produce trade signals from market data, rules and trained models. A customer
does not originate the trade idea. Crypto is signals-only and has no order path
at all.

### Three execution modes, and the distinction between them is the crux

| Mode | Behaviour | Human approves each trade? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `manual` | A signal is recorded and displayed. Nothing executes. | n/a — nothing executes |
| `copilot` | A signal is queued for approval. A human approves or rejects each one. | **Yes** |
| `autopilot` | `_execute_signal(signal, approved_by="autopilot")` runs with no per-trade human step. Risk gates apply; a person does not. | **No** |

The default is `manual` (`ExecutionModeManager.__init__`). Autopilot is a
deliberate operator action.

**This table is the question.** `copilot` is plausibly non-discretionary order
tooling; `autopilot` places orders in a customer's account, selected by the
system, without that customer seeing them first. Counsel should assume both
modes will be offered unless told otherwise, and classify them separately if
they fall differently.

### What risk controls exist

Every order path passes one gate chain in `_execute_signal`: kill switch,
market hours, 0DTE restrictions, liquidity and gamma checks, guardrails, trade
frequency, strategy health, and a portfolio concentration gate. These are
engineering controls. Whether they are *supervisory* controls in the regulatory
sense is part of what is being asked.

### What is NOT true today, and must not be assumed

- **A stored customer connection does not route that customer's orders.**
  Execution still uses a process-wide broker singleton selected from the
  operator's own environment (`broker_factory.get_broker()`, 52 call sites).
  Today every order goes to the operator's configured account.
- Paper is the default (`ibkr_trading_mode=paper`; the default Alpaca base URL
  is the paper host). Live requires a deliberate switch.
- `EXECUTION_ENABLED=false` makes a deployment structurally unable to place
  any order.
- There is no validated live track record. The product says so on its own
  landing page.

## The classification being asked for

§2.2 lists five, and more than one may apply, possibly differently per mode:

1. analytics and self-directed decision support;
2. non-discretionary order tooling;
3. customer-authorized automated trading;
4. discretionary investment management or robo-advice;
5. order routing or another broker-dealer activity.

Primary guidance named in §2.2:

- [SEC investment-adviser registration and Form ADV](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-investment-management/electronic-filing-investment-advisers-iard/electronic-filing-investment-advisers-iard-how-register-sec-investment-adviser-how-file-reports-sec)
- [SEC Rule 15c3-5 market-access controls](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2011/06/risk-management-controls-brokers-or-dealers-market-access)
- [FINRA algorithmic-trading supervision and control guidance](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/algorithmic-trading)

## Questions counsel's answer needs to settle

Listed because each one changes something concrete in the build, not as a
general survey.

1. **Does offering `autopilot` to a customer change the classification versus
   offering `copilot` alone?** If yes, the two modes may need different
   eligibility, disclosure or entitlement, and that is an engineering change in
   `execution_mode.py` and the entitlements layer.
2. **Is a multi-member organization materially different from a personal one?**
   One person trading in another's account may be a different activity.
   ADR-0001 deliberately shipped personal organizations only, so that this
   question could be answered without re-keying anything.
3. **What must be disclosed, where, and with what acknowledgement?** A
   disclosure that must be accepted before live trading is a gated flow with a
   recorded, timestamped consent, not a paragraph on a page.
4. **What recordkeeping is required, for how long?** §14.3's retention and
   §15.4's tamper-evident audit should be sized against a real obligation
   rather than a guess.
5. **Does supervision require a named human able to intervene, and within what
   time?** That is the difference between a kill switch and a staffed desk, and
   it sets Phase 2 action 5's requirements.
6. **Are the existing risk gates sufficient as supervisory controls**, or is
   something specific required that does not exist?
7. **Does any of this require registration**, and if so, what must be true of
   the system before customer onboarding begins?
8. **What may marketing say?** The charter already forbids performance claims;
   counsel should confirm what positive statements are permitted.

## Consequences of leaving this open

Phase 2 action 2 — routing a customer's orders through their own connection —
is the first change that makes the platform do the thing being classified. It
stays unbuilt until this is answered. Everything merged to date is inert or
behaviour-preserving, specifically so that this question gates nothing already
shipped.

Deciding it late is cheap now and expensive later: the schema shape, the
disclosure flow, the audit retention and the supervision model all follow from
it, and all are harder to change once a customer's money is in them.

## What engineering will do with the answer

- Record it here as Accepted, with the classification stated plainly.
- Raise follow-up ADRs for anything it mandates (retention, audit immutability,
  identity provider, disclosure flow).
- Only then begin Phase 2 action 2, under the pilot scope defined by §23 item 8.
